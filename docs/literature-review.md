## Corrections and additions to the topic brief

1. **"Billed at a tenth of the normal input price" was true at launch and is out of date now.** DeepSeek announced context caching on disk on 2 August 2024 at $0.014 per million cache-hit tokens against $0.14 for misses, a tenth, with cache storage free of charge. On the Models & Pricing page read on 3 November 2026, a cache hit on `deepseek-flash` (served by DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017) costs $0.006 per million tokens at peak and $0.003 off-peak, against $0.30 and $0.15 for a miss: a fiftieth. On `deepseek-v4-pro` a hit costs $0.044 and $0.022 against $1.32 and $0.66, a thirtieth. Off-peak prices are half the peak prices; peak hours are 01:00 to 04:00 and 06:00 to 10:00 UTC on weekdays. A sentence we can defend: "DeepSeek has served context caching on disk since August 2024; a cache hit cost a tenth of a miss at launch and costs a fiftieth on V4.1-Flash today." The widening gap fits the brief's argument that smaller caches make persistence cheaper, but a price is not a cost, and the paper should not treat it as one.

2. **The 64-token unit and the drop from 13 s to 0.5 s belong to the 2024 system.** Both appear on the August 2024 page, which says the cache stores entries in 64-token units and that the first-token latency of a 128K prompt with heavy repetition fell from 13 s to 500 ms, and which credits the MLA architecture of DeepSeek-V2 for making disk storage practical. The same page says unused entries are usually cleared within a few hours to a few days. The V4.1-Flash system described in the DeepInfer notes matches prefixes in 128-token blocks. The brief should date these numbers to 2024.

3. **DeepInfer is real and correctly dated, but the cache it describes is not part of the release.** The repository `deepseek-ai/DeepInfer` (Apache 2.0) was created on 16 October 2026 and received its first commit on 20 October. It is an inference engine for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. Its README lists a KV cache system first among the parts that a complete service needs and that this release does not include, and the deployment Q&A (`doc/inference_deployment_qa.md`) says the KV cache management and load-balancing code is not yet fully open-sourced. The description of the cache sits in that Q&A: the 3FS file system provides roughly 100 PiB of SSD for the KV cache, with a time-to-live of 3 days for agent traffic and 7 days for chatbot traffic, and a TTL of about 3 days costs chatbot traffic 0.5% to 1% of its hit rate. The brief's figures are right, and decode-time KV is written to disk, as the brief says. Four further details matter for the cost model. Reclamation is LRU garbage collection in 3FS that keeps disk usage below a watermark, with no expiry logic in the cache store itself, so the TTLs are retention targets rather than hard expiries. A recommended storage server holds 20 SSDs of 30.73 TB and delivers at least 40 GB/s of reads and 5 GB/s of writes, with capacity for 24 hours of data. The cluster provisions about 14.4 TB of logical cache per GPU. And one of the system's three design goals is to retain KV cache for more than 72 hours. Suggested wording: "DeepSeek open-sourced its DeepInfer engine on 20 October; the accompanying deployment notes describe, but do not release, the cache behind its API: about 100 PiB of SSD, with entries kept for three days for agent traffic and seven for chat."

4. **"Sliding-window state is rebuilt by replaying the last 128 tokens instead of being stored" is half right.** In V4.1-Flash every layer combines compressed global attention with sliding-window attention (SWA). According to the V4.1 report (section 3.2) and the DeepInfer notes, the SWA state of the first 20 layers (the causal encoder) is stored, but in a distributed pool of host DRAM (10% of each machine's memory) with a TTL of minutes, not on SSD: 20 layers × 134 tokens × 528 bytes = 1,415,040 bytes per sequence, saved at multiples of 128 tokens. Replaying the last 128 tokens is the fallback when that state has expired, and it is the only path for the 20 decoder layers' SWA state, which is never stored. The replay truncates the window, so it rebuilds an approximate state; DeepSeek reports a negligible effect on quality, which we have not checked. This is the closest published example of a per-layer store-or-replay decision with different retention for each kind of state, and the cost model should reproduce it (section 3).

5. **The 890 bytes per token are right and are the right number for disk, but the 590-fold range mixes precision with architecture.** The V4.1 report gives 890 bytes per token as the global KV footprint, which always stays in HBM: main KV and indexer keys in FP4, shared across layers and compressed along the sequence. Because V4.1 no longer persists SWA state, the same global KV is what goes to SSD, and the report puts the persistent footprint at about 1/8 of V4-Flash's. Our reading of the model's `config.json` reproduces the 890 bytes exactly: four KV-source layers (three in the encoder with a sequence compression ratio of 2, one in the decoder with ratio 1), each entry holding 288 bytes of FP4 main KV (512 values plus one E4M3 scale per 16) and 68 bytes of MXFP4 indexer key (128 values plus one scale per 32). The brief's other figures are also correct at 16 bits per value: 512 KiB per token for a Llama-2-7B-shaped model, 128 KiB for Llama-3.1-8B and 31,104 bytes (30.4 KiB) for DeepSeek-V2-Lite's latent. The ratio 524,288 / 890 = 589.1 is correct arithmetic, but V4.1's global KV stored at 16 bits would take 3,200 bytes (our computation), so a factor of about 164 comes from architecture and 3.6 from precision. The paper should state the precision on both sides or compare at equal precision.

6. **Bytes per token does not decide on its own.** Loading a token's cache takes about b/B seconds for b bytes at bandwidth B; recomputing it takes about 1/R at a prefill rate of R tokens per second; and every hit pays a fixed cost. Ibrahim's break-even bandwidth B* = sR (reference [4]) says this directly, and EfficientAgent (reference [7]) finds that offloading pays on GPUs with little peak compute per byte of host bandwidth. The natural axis is therefore bytes per token divided by recompute time per token, a ratio in the spirit of a roofline: the decision flips where the storage path's bandwidth crosses it, as a kernel flips from memory-bound to compute-bound at the ridge point. Compute per token also varies by design: V4.1-Flash activates 8B parameters per token during prefill, DeepSeek-V2-Lite 2.4B and a Llama-2-7B-shaped model about 7B. The roofline framing should be familiar to the student from the Kernel Engineering workshop.

7. **Reference [4] is summarized correctly, with three caveats.** The paper (Ibrahim, 15 October 2026) models T_load = T0 + ns/B and T_recompute = n/R for GLM-5.3, an FP8 mixture of experts with MLA, at s = 48.4 KB per token and R = 52.2k tokens per second on one 8×B300 node with prefill context parallelism, which gives B* = 2.53 GB/s and T0 ≈ 0.33 s. The caveats: the tier is a remote Valkey key-value store reached over the network, not an SSD; T0 is fitted on a single point, and the author states that the implied 17k-token threshold has not been measured directly; and the latency results cover one bucket of prompt lengths (14.7k to 17.9k tokens). The paper also makes a point the brief should keep: below the threshold a hit still has value as capacity, because it uses no GPU compute, which matters when prefill engines are saturated. It is a single-author preprint.

8. **References [5] to [8] are summarized correctly, and two of them overlap the research questions more than the brief says.** py-kvcache [5] already gates loads with a break-even prefix length per node, model and source tier, calibrated offline from measured Pareto frontiers. On its H100 node the SSD break-even for Qwen3-4B was 6,203 tokens, and the average request of the Bailian traces fell below it. That is a calibrated, per-model answer to RQ1; ours has to predict across models without recalibration. Gandalf [8] already admits full-attention KV to SSD ahead of linear-attention and sliding-window state, using an SSD lifetime model (rated terabytes written divided by write amplification times workload writes) and an oracle that optimizes admission and eviction jointly; it extends SSD lifetime 7.3 times on average with a negligible effect on hit rate. That covers much of RQ2 for hybrid models and the endurance term of RQ3. The placement study [6] is a discrete-event simulation whose headline gain (73.02 times more concurrent sessions per GPU) equals the assumed tier capacity multiple of 1+8+64, which its authors describe as a matter of capacity accounting rather than placement policy. EfficientAgent [7] concerns a tier in host DRAM, not on SSD.

9. **"None covers several attention designs in one model while deciding per layer what to store, what to replay and for how long" is overstated.** For one model, the V4.1 report and the DeepInfer notes decide per kind of state what goes to SSD (global KV, kept at least 72 hours), what stays briefly in DRAM (encoder SWA state, minutes) and what is replayed (decoder SWA state always, encoder SWA state on a miss). Gandalf decides per attention type what to admit to SSD and counts endurance. AnchorKV (28 October) derives, from the config alone, at which layer spacing stored hidden-state anchors beat stored KV; HCache (EuroSys 2025) splits layers between loading KV and rebuilding it from hidden states; SuffixReplay and HiState handle linear-attention state by replaying a suffix or by checkpointing to SSD; and Marconi (MLSys 2025) admits and evicts hybrid-model states by compute saved per byte. What we did not find, as of 3 November, is one cost model, validated on measurements, that takes bytes per token, the recompute curve, the per-hit cost, capacity-time and endurance as inputs, predicts the persist-or-replay decision across MHA, GQA, MLA, compressed and hybrid designs on the same hardware, and expresses it in dollars per million input tokens. The brief should claim that and no more.

10. **RQ1's target, the break-even length within about 15%, is ill-conditioned.** Ignoring the quadratic attention term, the break-even length is L* = T0 / (1/R − b/B). When b/B is close to 1/R, a small error in B produces a large error in L*: at b/B = 0.8/R, a 5% error in B moves L* by 16% to 27%, depending on its sign. When b/B exceeds 1/R there is no break-even until attention's quadratic cost catches up. We propose to score the model by its decisions instead: the share of (model, length, tier) cells in which it picks the faster option, the regret in seconds of time to first token when it picks wrong, and the error of its predicted hit latency. The break-even length can still be reported where it is well defined.

11. **The public traces are too short for three- and seven-day retention.** Mooncake's FAST'25 traces (Apache 2.0) are sampled from one hour of requests (0.98 hours by our computation) and hash prefixes in 512-token blocks. Alibaba's Bailian traces cover two hours in 16-token blocks, and the SemiAnalysis Claude Code traces that Gandalf used cover one day in 64-token blocks (all Apache 2.0). None of them can test a 3- or 7-day TTL; the only published day-scale data point is DeepSeek's. Within an hour, reuse is fast. With unlimited capacity, 37.4% of the input tokens of Mooncake's conversation trace are prefix hits if nothing ever expires, 29.3% with a 5-minute idle TTL and 36.2% with 15 minutes; on the tool-and-agent trace the figures are 57.1%, 52.9% and 56.9% (our computation). RQ3 should either model the long tail explicitly, and say that it does, or restrict itself to retention from minutes to a day.

12. **The Indian-language clause reduces to a length effect.** Per token, the decision does not depend on the language: bytes per token and the recompute rate belong to the model. A language that needs more tokens per word makes each conversation longer, so it reaches the break-even length sooner, spreads the fixed per-hit cost over more tokens and raises the quadratic share of recompute. The length axis already captures all three, and tokenizer fertility converts tokens to words (the topic 24 review measured Qwen3-8B's tokenizer at 4.1 times the English token count for Hindi and 9.0 times for Odia on Belebele prompts). We suggest keeping multi-turn chats in Indian languages as a workload, reported per word, and dropping the clause from RQ3.

13. **Models and access.** Meta's Llama-2-7B and Llama-3.1-8B repositories are gated with manual approval on Hugging Face, and the Modal workspace has no Hugging Face token yet (topic 15 run log, 3 November). Ungated models with the needed cache shapes exist. `lmsys/longchat-7b-v1.5-32k` is a LlamaForCausalLM model with 32 layers and 32 KV heads of 128 dimensions and linear RoPE scaling to 32k tokens, so 512 KiB per token at 16 bits; its card names Apache 2.0 and says nothing else, and we did not check its provenance. `Qwen/Qwen3-8B` (GQA, 144 KiB per token at 16 bits) is already in the topic 15 Modal volume, and `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat` (MLA, 30.4 KiB; DeepSeek's model licence) is ungated. For sliding-window and hybrid layers, `openai/gpt-oss-20b` (Apache 2.0) alternates 12 full-attention layers with 12 sliding-window layers of 128 tokens, the same window as V4.1, and `Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B` (Apache 2.0) has 8 full-attention layers and 24 Gated DeltaNet layers whose recurrent state, kept in float32 as the config specifies, takes about 49 MiB per sequence checkpoint, as much as the full-attention KV of about 1,570 tokens (our computation from the configs).

14. **The host: what Modal offers in place of "at least 1 TB of local NVMe".** A Modal container gets a worker-local SSD with a 512 GiB default quota, which the `ephemeral_disk` setting raises to at most 3.0 TiB; disk is billed by raising the memory request at a ratio of 20 to 1. The disk disappears when the container stops, so it cannot hold a cache for days. Modal does not document its read speed, and Modal's security page says every container runs in its own sandbox built on gVisor or microVMs, so `O_DIRECT`, page-cache behaviour and GPUDirect Storage may not behave as they do on bare metal. Persistent storage is a Volume, a distributed network file system that Modal designs for up to 2.5 GB/s and does not guarantee. The brief's first risk is therefore real on Modal: the local option is ephemeral and the persistent option is network-attached. Exp01 measures both on day 1. SSD wear also cannot be observed from inside a sandbox, so endurance has to be modelled from bytes written and a stated drive rating.

15. **Software versions.** The current stable releases are vLLM v0.32.0 (18 October 2026) and LMCache 0.5.6 (15 October; 0.5.7 is in release candidates). LMCache's documentation marks the in-process mode that hosts its local-disk backend as deprecated in favour of a multi-process mode, and LMCache does not store decode-time KV by default (`save_decode_cache: false`), whereas DeepSeek does. py-kvcache measured LMCache's disk path at half its own speed at 80k tokens, and Strata reports up to 5 times lower time to first token than vLLM with LMCache, so "LMCache on disk" as a baseline measures LMCache's implementation as much as the hardware; vLLM's native offloading and py-kvcache (Apache 2.0) are useful second implementations.

16. **An addition for the student: where scheduling and admission enter.** The cost model is not only an offline calculator; it answers five decisions that a scheduler makes. (a) Load or recompute, per request, at schedule time: py-kvcache starts disk reads while a request is still queued, and CachedAttention fetches by hints from the job scheduler. (b) The price of recompute under load: when prefill engines are saturated, a recompute delays other tenants, so a hit is worth more than its latency gain (Ibrahim's capacity point; Continuum sets KV time-to-live from reload cost and queueing delay). (c) Stability: synchronous loads behave like a single-server queue with utilization λns/B, and above 1 the queue grows without bound (Ibrahim measured a p99 of 83.32 s with the GPUs 7% busy). (d) Admission and retention per tenant and traffic class: DeepSeek retains agent traffic for 3 days and chat for 7, and PrefixShield (August 2026) charges each tenant group for the cache blocks it materializes. (e) Routing and capacity: a shared storage tier lets any prefill engine hit any prefix, so DeepSeek routes without prefix affinity and sizes prefill capacity by non-cached tokens, and Mooncake's scheduler is built around the KV cache, with early rejection to protect SLOs. An SLO-aware persist-or-replay policy is the natural second paper, and the measurements of this one are its inputs.

## 1. Positioning

A serving system that has computed a prompt's KV cache can keep it and load it back later, or discard it and replay the prompt when it returns. The choice is as old as paged KV caches: the vLLM paper already recovered preempted requests either by swapping their blocks to CPU memory or by recomputing them, and found that the better option depends on the block size. Production systems then turned the choice into a storage tier: DeepSeek's disk cache since August 2024, Mooncake for Kimi, LMCache, SGLang's HiCache and NVIDIA Dynamo's KV block manager. Since September 2026 a burst of papers has asked when such a tier pays. A remote tier on B300 GPUs turned out slower than recompute below about 17k tokens (Ibrahim). vLLM on NVMe needs a calibrated break-even length per model (py-kvcache). A host tier pays for agents only when it holds the pool's reuse working set (EfficientAgent). SSD writes need an endurance budget, and hybrid models should send only full-attention KV to flash (Gandalf). Hidden-state anchors save memory only beyond a spacing computable from the config (AnchorKV). And DeepSeek published a production design that stores, keeps briefly or replays each kind of state differently (V4.1-Flash). Each of these answers the question for one model, one tier or one mechanism.

The claim this paper can defend is a unifying one. A cost model with a handful of measured terms (bytes per token, the prefill time curve, effective storage bandwidth and a fixed per-hit cost, plus capacity-time and endurance prices) predicts the persist-or-replay decision for attention designs it was not fitted on, on the same hardware, and its per-layer and retention recommendations reproduce the choices that DeepSeek, Gandalf and AnchorKV made separately. The deliverables are the model, one set of measurements on one GPU across MHA, GQA and MLA (with sliding-window and hybrid layers as a second step), and an open calculator. It is a measurement-and-model paper in the style of a roofline analysis, a good fit for CHEOPS (storage) and EuroMLSys (ML systems). A clean negative result, that per-hit costs are too implementation-specific for bytes per token to transfer across designs, would also be publishable, as a warning about the many single-model break-even numbers now in print.

Correction 16 lists where the model meets scheduling. For the student, the most direct route from this paper to his first interest is to put the model's per-request prediction inside an SLO-aware multi-tenant scheduler, which section 8 raises as a scoping question.

## 2. Related work

Nine clusters, closest work first within each, and the clusters themselves ordered roughly by how close they come to our question. The tags: *closest* or *closer* (overlaps our question), *complementary* (we use a piece of it, or it constrains our design), *baseline* (we run or compare against it), *production* (a deployed system's own description), *testbed* (models, traces and benchmarks) and *tool* (software we build on).

### 2.1 Load or recompute: cost models and break-even points

- **When the KV Cache Is Slower Than the GPU: Scaling Remote KV Tiers for Context-Parallel Prefill.** Muhammad Ibrahim, 2026 (15 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21500. *Closest.* The brief's [4]. A two-term model, T_load = T0 + ns/B against T_recompute = n/R, explains why a remote LMCache tier backed by a Valkey store left the median time to first token unchanged but raised the 16k-token p99 from 3.96 s to 17.3 s for GLM-5.3 on an 8×B300 node: below B* = sR = 2.53 GB/s every hit loses, and a fixed engine cost of about 0.33 s puts prompts under about 17k tokens out of reach at any bandwidth (corrections, item 7). Sharding the store by context-parallel rank raised GET bandwidth 6.3 times to 10.76 GB/s and cut the hit-heavy p99 from 83.32 s to 0.67 s. Our model adds the attention term of recompute, a per-chunk cost, capacity-time and endurance, and tests transfer across designs; their two terms are the baseline model in ablation A1.
- **Building py-kvcache: A Performance Characterization of External KV Caching for vLLM with NVMe SSDs.** Joseph Kanichai, Tiziano De Matteis and Animesh Trivedi, 2026 (10 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11744. *Closest (measurement).* The brief's [5]. Characterizes LMCache, vLLM's native KV offload and llm-d's file-system connector on NVMe drives that read 13.5 GB/s in fio, and finds that transfer granularity, staging memory and the moment transfers enter the schedule matter as much as device bandwidth. Its connector, py-kvcache (Apache 2.0), loads from disk 2.0 times faster than LMCache at 80k tokens (preloading while a request waits contributes 1.34 times) and gates loads by an offline-calibrated break-even prefix length per node, model and tier; for Qwen3-4B on an H100 node the SSD break-even was 6,203 tokens. The same question as Exp01, answered per model by calibration; we must predict across models, and its pitfalls (oversized staging buffers, small transfers, page cache) are a checklist for our harness.
- **CacheFlow: Efficient LLM Serving via Automated 3D-Parallel KV Cache Restoration.** Sean Nian, Hanzhang Shen, Zhiyu Wu et al., 2026 (28 April; v2, 26 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25080. *Closer.* Treats restoration as a parallel execution problem across tokens, layers and GPUs: a batch-aware dynamic-programming planner decides what to recompute and what to load across requests and layer blocks, for dense and hybrid models; the abstract reports 2.24 to 3.00 times lower restoration latency on average than earlier methods. Its planner balances GPU computation against I/O, the trade-off our model prices; whether that trade-off can be predicted from bytes per token across designs is the question we add.
- **EfficientAgent: What Makes KV Cache Offloading Work for Concurrent Agents?** Kunming Shao, Jierun Chen, Jiangnan Yu et al., 2026 (27 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33762. *Closer.* The brief's [7]. Separates what a host-tier hit is worth (set by peak GPU compute per byte of host-link bandwidth) from whether the hit happens (set by the agent pool's reuse working set, estimated with a stack-distance model). On SWE-bench Verified agents with Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B on eight H20 GPUs, a host tier sized to the working set cut computed prefill by 93.1% and makespan by 38.7%, while offloading slowed every run on H800 GPUs, whose compute per host byte is higher. Host DRAM, not SSD, but the same ratio as our break-even and a working-set view of retention.
- **Compute Or Load KV Cache? Why Not Both?** Shuowei Jin, Xueshen Liu, Qingzhao Zhang and Z. Morley Mao, 2024. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03065. *Closer.* Cake computes part of the prefix's KV while loading the rest, with a bidirectional schedule that adapts the split to the available compute and I/O; the abstract reports an average 2.6 times lower time to first token than compute-only or load-only restoration. Persist or replay need not be all or nothing: with the two running in parallel, the restore time is roughly L divided by the sum of the recompute rate and B/b (our reading), a case our model should include.
- **KVPR: Efficient LLM Inference with I/O-Aware KV Cache Partial Recomputation.** Chaoyi Jiang, Lei Gao, Hossein Entezari Zarch and Murali Annavaram, 2024. ACL Findings 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17089. *Complementary.* Transfers part of the activations first so that the GPU recomputes part of the KV while the rest crosses PCIe from CPU memory. The same overlap idea for a host tier.
- **CacheGen: KV Cache Compression and Streaming for Fast Large Language Model Serving.** Yuhan Liu, Hanchen Li, Yihua Cheng et al., 2023. SIGCOMM 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240. *Complementary.* Encodes KV caches 3.5 to 4.3 times smaller and adapts the compression level of each part of the context to the available bandwidth. Compression moves b, the first term of our model.
- **Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention.** Woosuk Kwon, Zhuohan Li, Siyuan Zhuang et al., 2023. SOSP 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180. *Baseline (engine).* Paged KV blocks and prefix sharing. Its section 7.3 compares swapping preempted blocks to CPU memory with recomputing them on OPT-13B: recomputation wins at small block sizes, swapping at large ones, with comparable results for blocks of 16 to 64 tokens. The original persist-or-replay measurement, already showing that transfer granularity decides; vLLM's prefix caching is Exp01's GPU-only baseline.
- **FlexGen: High-Throughput Generative Inference of Large Language Models with a Single GPU.** Ying Sheng, Lianmin Zheng, Binhang Yuan et al., 2023. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06865. *Complementary.* Searches placements of weights, activations and KV cache across GPU, CPU and disk by solving a linear program over a cost model. An early example of choosing placement from bandwidth and compute terms, aimed at throughput rather than reuse.

### 2.2 DeepSeek's disk cache and the V4.1 design

- **DeepSeek API introduces Context Caching on Disk, cutting prices by an order of magnitude.** DeepSeek, 2 August 2024. API documentation (news). https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news0802. *Production.* The brief's [1]: prefixes cached on a distributed disk array in 64-token units, hits at $0.014 per million tokens against $0.14, first-token latency for a 128K prompt down from 13 s to 0.5 s, entries cleared within hours to days, credited to MLA (corrections, items 1 and 2).
- **Models & Pricing.** DeepSeek API documentation, read 3 November 2026. https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing. *Production.* Current prices: a cache hit on V4.1-Flash costs a fiftieth of a miss, on V4-Pro a thirtieth, with off-peak rates at half the peak rates (item 1).
- **DeepInfer and its deployment Q&A.** DeepSeek-AI, 2026 (first commit 20 October). GitHub, Apache 2.0. https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer and https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer/blob/main/doc/inference_deployment_qa.md. *Production (the closest description of a deployed persist-or-replay system).* The brief's [2]. Besides the cache facts in items 3 and 4, the notes say a single cluster serves 155 trillion tokens a day at a revenue of $38 per GPU-hour at off-peak pricing, and describe a prefill scheduler that sizes each chunk with a fitted quadratic cost model of new tokens and cached prefix length, prefix matching over a metadata hash chain that must finish before routing, asynchronous fetches from 3FS into host shared memory, and a layer-by-layer swap into HBM over GPUDirect RDMA. The Zhihu post the Q&A builds on was not read.
- **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression.** DeepSeek-AI, 2026 (17 September). arXiv technical report. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969. *Closest (design).* The brief's [3]. A 552B-parameter mixture of experts with a causal encoder-decoder (8B active parameters per token in prefill, 16B in decode), cross-layer KV reuse in Compressed Sparse Attention 2 and FP4 global KV at 890 bytes per token. Section 3.2 describes the persistent cache: in V4 the SWA state took nearly half of the SSD cache although it is reused only within minutes, while global KV has a long tail of reuse, so V4.1 keeps global KV on SSD for at least 72 hours, moves SWA state to a host-DRAM pool with a TTL of minutes, and replays a bounded 128-token window on a miss. The report argues the trade-off in prose and publishes no cost model; ours should recover its choices from the numbers.
- **DeepSeek-V4: Towards Highly Efficient Million-Token Context Intelligence.** DeepSeek-AI, 2026. arXiv technical report. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348. *Complementary.* Cited by the V4.1 report for V4's compressed attention (CSA and HCA) and for "Zero SWA Caching", which recomputed missing SWA state exactly at the cost of a forward pass over L × n_win tokens, which the V4.1 report says was too expensive in production. We have not read it.
- **DeepSeek-V2: A Strong, Economical, and Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Language Model.** DeepSeek-AI, 2024. arXiv technical report. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434. *Testbed (design).* Introduces multi-head latent attention, whose small per-token latent the 2024 announcement credits for making disk caching practical; DeepSeek-V2-Lite is Exp01's MLA model.

### 2.3 Per-layer store or replay

- **AnchorKV: Lossless Key-Value Cache Reconstruction from Layer-Sparse Residual Anchors.** Hao Li, Jiashu Zhang, Kaleem Ullah Qasim and Heying Zhang, 2026 (28 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00117. *Closest (per layer).* A storage-replay spectrum: keep the residual stream entering every s-th layer and replay the layers in between. Anchors save memory over KV only when s exceeds d / (2·n_kv·d_head), a ratio computable from the config (0.50 to 3.50 across the six families it audits; 2.00 for Qwen3-8B), so caching hidden states halves memory only for MHA-like shapes. Interleaved sliding-window models get anchors placed by dynamic programming, and reconstruction is bit-exact. It optimizes capacity, not time on a disk tier and not dollars; our per-layer analysis (ablation A3) starts from its rule and asks whether the best spacing moves with bandwidth.
- **Fast State Restoration in LLM Serving with HCache.** Shiwei Gao, Youmin Chen and Jiwu Shu, 2024. EuroSys 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05004. *Closer.* Restores state from stored hidden states, recomputing K and V by projection, and schedules layers between this and plain KV loading; the abstract reports up to 1.93 times lower time to first token than KV offload with 1.92 to 2.40 times less storage. Its storage saving is the MHA case of AnchorKV's rule: a Llama-2-7B-shaped layer stores 8 KiB of hidden state per token against 16 KiB of KV, but a Qwen3-8B layer stores 8 KiB against 4 KiB and a DeepSeek-V2-Lite layer 4 KiB against 1.1 KiB (our computation).
- **Just Let Linear States Forget the Distant Past: Prefix Caching via Suffix Replay for Hybrid LLMs.** Yirui Liu, Ruoling Qi, Xuaner Wu et al., 2026 (27 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33477. *Closest (hybrid layers).* SuffixReplay rebuilds linear-attention state at any cached page boundary by replaying a short recent suffix of retained hidden-state anchors, instead of storing recurrent checkpoints, with a bounded replay budget; evaluated on OLMo-Hybrid-7B, Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.6-27B-FP8. The brief's plan to extend bounded replay to hybrid layers is, in effect, this paper.
- **Tail-Replay: Escaping the Curse of Linear Attention in Prefix Caching for Hybrid LLMs.** Yirui Liu, Ruoling Qi, Xuaner Wu et al., 2026 (31 August; v2, 15 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30310. *Closer.* The same group's earlier version: cache the exact full-attention KV, drop recurrent checkpoints and approximate the linear-attention state by replaying a short tail of the matched prefix, because gated recurrent updates attenuate old inputs.
- **HiState: Hierarchical Prefix Cache Management for Hybrid LLM Serving in Agentic Workloads.** Zaifeng Pan, Lucas Wang, Xinwei Qiang et al., 2026 (30 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41508. *Closer.* A hit needs every matching KV entry but only one recurrent checkpoint, so HiState keeps historical checkpoints on SSD and KV in fast memory, with adaptive checkpoint intervals that balance checkpoint storage against recomputation; on two public coding-agent traces the abstract reports p99 time to first token up to 5.73 times lower than vLLM with Marconi and 5.44 times lower than SGLang's HiCache. The checkpoint-spacing trade-off of our RQ2, solved for hybrid agents.
- **Marconi: Prefix Caching for the Era of Hybrid LLMs.** Rui Pan, Zhuang Wang, Zhen Jia et al., 2024. MLSys 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19379. *Closer.* Recurrent layers update state in place, so hybrid models get only exact-match hits and produce many large cache entries; Marconi admits and evicts by forecast reuse and by compute saved relative to memory footprint, the per-byte value our model computes. Up to 34.4 times higher token hit rates in the abstract.

### 2.4 SSD endurance and flash tiers

- **Gandalf: Flash Shall Not Die! Increasing Flash Storage Lifetime for KV Cache Offloading in LLM Serving.** Maharani A. P. Irawan, Kuntai Du, Jiayi Yao et al., 2026 (22 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30791. *Closest (endurance).* The brief's [8]. On two one-day SemiAnalysis Claude Code traces, representative hybrid models generate 81 to 240 TB of KV state per day, enough for even a 30-TB enterprise SSD to exhaust its rated writes in under a year. Because linear-attention and sliding-window state is typically used once, by the next turn, while full-attention KV is reused on every later turn, Gandalf admits full-attention KV to SSD first and decides between dropping and moving each DRAM victim by its expected reuse against the SSD write cost, extending SSD lifetime 7.3 times on average. Its lifetime formula is our endurance term; its admission order is a choice our model should predict from bytes and reuse alone.
- **SARIA: Persistence-Aware KV Caching with Coordinated DRAM–SSD Reload for LLM Agents.** Hui Li, Qihui Zhou, Xiao Yan et al., 2026 (22 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31169. *Closer.* Persists to SSD only blocks that show reuse beyond their DRAM residency, which saves writes and endurance, and paces DRAM and SSD reloads so that both share GPU ingress bandwidth; built on LMCache's multi-process mode and tested on full-attention and hybrid models, it cuts SSD writes by 57.1% to 58.9% against caching every block and mean time to first token by 42.3% to 79.7% against a DRAM-only cache (abstract). Selective persistence by reuse, without an explicit cost model.
- **Lachesis: Lifetime-Aware KV Cache Placement for Agent Serving across HBM and High-Bandwidth Flash.** Jaehoon Yang, Jeongmin Lee, Haneul Park et al., 2026 (6 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08378. *Complementary.* Places each KV segment in HBM or high-bandwidth flash by a lifetime inferred from the agent harness, so short-lived data absorbs writes in HBM; in trace-driven simulation flash lifetime grows 1.19 to 3.13 times, to 3.3 to 12.2 device-years. Lifetime as a placement signal, which our retention term generalizes to price.
- **FlashAccel: Leveraging High-Bandwidth Flash (HBF) for High-Throughput LLM Inference.** Xinyu Wang, Yalong Xue, Xiaotian Sun et al., 2026 (11 July). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10186. *Complementary.* Integrates high-bandwidth flash next to HBM; six stacks give 2.49 times the throughput per GPU of an HBM-only design under a 100 ms latency constraint. Gandalf describes it as trading retention for endurance because KV caches live briefly. A tier where B rises by an order of magnitude and endurance dominates, which the calculator should be able to represent.

### 2.5 Admission, eviction and retention

- **Understanding KV Residency in Production-Scale Agentic LLM Workloads.** Mayank Arya, Agrim Bari, Anjaly Parayil et al. (Microsoft), 2026 (16 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22863. *Closer (retention).* Four production M365 traces with more than 121 million model calls. About 80% of context tokens come from the prefix cache; holding every live session at its peak would need an estimated 501.8 TB of HBM (with Llama-2-70B's KV layout standing in), and a one-hour idle cap cuts that by 3.6 times while the share of calls that recompute context rises only from 4.07% to 4.53% (abstract). Retention in GPU memory, measured on production traffic; ours prices it on disk.
- **When Fancy Eviction Fails: Rethinking Cache Replacement For LLM Prefix Reuse.** Yiyu Liu, Minlan Yu and Juncheng Yang, 2026 (24 September; v2, 1 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28870. *Closer.* On production traces from two companies, 14 eviction algorithms barely beat LRU because active sessions return at regular intervals; misses vary widely in cost because attention grows with length, which the authors capture in a compute-savings ratio. They plan to release traces and a simulator, which would help RQ3.
- **KVCache Cache in the Wild: Characterizing and Optimizing KVCache Cache at a Large Cloud Provider.** Jiahao Wang, Jinbo Han, Xingda Wei et al., 2025. USENIX ATC 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02634. *Closer (workload).* The first characterization of KV-cache reuse at a large provider (Alibaba's Bailian): reuse is skewed, reuse between single-turn requests matters as much as multi-turn reuse, reuse time and probability are predictable within a request category, and the cache needed for an ideal hit ratio is moderate. Its traces are public for two hours.
- **Continuum: Efficient and Robust Multi-Turn LLM Agent Scheduling with KV Cache Time-to-Live.** Hanchen Li, Runyuan He, Qiuyang Mang et al., 2025 (v7, 8 September 2026). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02230. *Closer (retention and scheduling).* Pins an agent's KV in GPU memory across a tool call with a TTL set by reload cost and the queueing delay that eviction would cause; with program-level first-come-first-serve it improves average job completion time by over 8 times. A TTL derived from a cost model, in GPU memory and over seconds; ours is on disk and over hours to days.
- **WAKMan: Workload Aware KVCache Manager.** Suhas Kumar Morisetty, Vaibhavi Lokegaonkar, Sachin Mathew Varghese and Wesley Harris, 2026 (26 October). arXiv preprint (work done during an internship at Capital One). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36355. *Complementary.* Across 20 traffic mixes no single eviction policy wins (LRU leads on agent-heavy mixes, S3-FIFO elsewhere), so WAKMan picks the policy from live traffic, improving hit rate over LRU by 5 points on average. Its Capital One affiliation shows the same question arising in financial services, the student's industry.
- **Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Agentic LLM Serving.** Jiakun Fan, Han Meng, Yuxuan Xia et al., 2026 (30 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00391. *Complementary.* Recomputing tokens near the end of a long history costs more than near the start, because their attention spans more history, so Penelope evicts from the beginning of the unshared history and keeps the cached suffix. A direct consequence of the quadratic term in our recompute model.
- **RR-Evict: Fine-Grained Prefix Cache Eviction beyond LRU for Agentic LLM Serving.** Zaifeng Pan, Chris Wu, Zhengding Hu et al., 2026 (26 September; v2, 2 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32278. *Complementary.* LRU evicts whole agent histories together because an agent's accesses refresh all its blocks at once; trimming a tail chunk from each idle agent in turn cuts p99 time to first token by up to 75.4% in SGLang. A policy baseline for trace replays in week 3.
- **Preserving Admission Responsibility in Multi-Tenant Large Language Model Prefix Caches.** Zhiyu Wang and Rajkumar Buyya, 2026 (3 August). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01657. *Complementary (multi-tenant).* PrefixShield meters the KV blocks each tenant group materializes and makes the group that created the debt supply the eviction victims; under one-touch pollution it restores a victim tenant's hit ratio from 4.92% to 84.87% in vLLM. The closest bridge from this topic to the student's multi-tenant interest.
- **The KV Cache Working Set: Online Capacity Planning for LLM Inference Systems.** Luchang Li, Shuaishuai Wang, Zhao Ruan et al., 2026 (23 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27746. *Complementary.* KVSET estimates hit rate at every cache capacity in one pass with Mattson's stack algorithm. With the mean-field LRU analysis below, the cheap way to turn a trace into a hit-rate-against-capacity curve for the cost model.
- **Multi-Turn LLM Conversations under the Least-Recently-Used Policy: Mean-Field Asymptotics and Hit Ratio Approximation.** Heyuan Yao, Chutong Gao, Yuan Lyu et al., 2026 (2 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02027. *Complementary.* A closed-form limit of the LRU hit ratio for growing multi-turn prefixes, validated on Qwen3-8B on Ascend NPUs; an analytical alternative to simulation for the retention term.
- **Where Should the KV Cache Live? Placement Policies Across GPU, CPU, and SSD for Long-Lived Sessions.** Srikanta Datta Tumkur, Jay Iyer, Mehar Simhadri et al., 2026 (14 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16215. *Complementary.* The brief's [6]. A simulator over HBM, DRAM and SSD finds that gains track tier capacity, not placement policy, that prefetching does not justify its bandwidth, and that its predicted-reuse policy is byte-identical to LRU (corrections, item 8).
- **MeritKV, Bidaw and TENURE.** Not checked: none of the three is on arXiv (title searches through the API returned nothing on 3 November). Gandalf's bibliography lists MeritKV (Khemani, Leri and Mittal, TMLR 2026: admit to storage only when saved prefill time exceeds lookup, transfer and expected miss costs), Bidaw (Hu et al., FAST 2026, weighing storage footprint against computation saved) and TENURE (Noohi and Barbalace, SoCC 2026, residency leases for tiered KV caches). All three sit close to our admission and retention terms and must be read from their venues in week 2.

### 2.6 Tiered KV cache systems

- **Mooncake: A KVCache-centric Disaggregated Architecture for LLM Serving.** Ruoyu Qin, Zheming Li, Weiran He, Mingxing Zhang et al., 2024 (v5, 8 October 2026). arXiv; the FAST 2025 version is titled "Mooncake: Trading More Storage for Less Computation". https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00079. *Baseline (system) and testbed (traces).* Kimi's serving platform: separate prefill and decode clusters and a disaggregated KV cache over the GPU cluster's CPU, DRAM and SSD, with a scheduler built around the KV cache that balances throughput against latency SLOs and rejects requests early under overload. Its FAST'25 traces are our trace source, and its subtitle states the brief's question in one line.
- **LMCache: An Efficient KV Cache Layer for Enterprise-Scale LLM Inference.** Yuhan Liu, Yihua Cheng, Jiayi Yao et al., 2025 (v2, 5 December 2025). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09665. *Tool and baseline.* The open-source KV cache layer for vLLM and SGLang, with CPU, local-disk and remote backends and a control API; the abstract reports up to 15 times higher throughput with vLLM and, from enterprise use, that context truncation can halve the prefix hit rate. Version 0.5.6 is the disk baseline of Exp01.
- **Cost-Efficient Large Language Model Serving for Multi-turn Conversations with CachedAttention.** Bin Gao, Zhuomin He, Puru Sharma et al., 2024. USENIX ATC 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19708. *Closer (system).* Hierarchical KV caching across memory and storage for multi-turn chats, with layer-wise preloading, asynchronous saving and fetching and eviction guided by the job scheduler; the abstract reports up to 87% lower time to first token and up to 70% lower end-to-end cost. The first system paper to argue that saving conversation KV to cheap storage beats recomputing it.
- **Strata: Hierarchical Context Caching for Long Context Language Model Serving.** Zhiqiang Xie, Ziyi Xu, Mark Zhao et al., 2025. arXiv preprint (under review). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18572. *Baseline (system).* GPU-assisted I/O against fragmented paged layouts and scheduling that accounts for cache-loading delays; deployed in production on SGLang, with up to 5 times lower time to first token than vLLM with LMCache in the abstract.
- **Range: Decoupling KV-Cache Reuse Granularity from Storage Work.** Dingsen Shi, Jiahao Zhang, Liqiang Lv et al., 2026 (15 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21241. *Complementary (an input to T0).* On a well-configured path the SSD is not the bottleneck: restoring the same 6 GiB takes 2.1 s as 192 objects and 3.0 s as 6,144, because every layer of the storage stack charges per object. Our fixed cost therefore needs a per-chunk part.
- **Characterizing Predictability-Latency Trade-offs of KV-Cache SSD Offloading in LMCache for LLM Serving Systems.** Ying He, Dingsen Shi, Yanbo Dai et al., 2026 (15 October). arXiv preprint (6 pages). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21249. *Complementary (method).* With buffered I/O, LMCache's SSD path looks almost read-free until the page cache is flushed, after which about 1 GB of reads takes 683 ms; O_DIRECT makes the cost explicit and stable but raises warm latency. The reason Exp01 needs a cold-read protocol.
- **FlatCake: A Flat KVCache Storage Architecture for LLM Serving.** Xin Huang, Teng Ma, Zhuoran Li et al., 2026 (28 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38355. *Complementary.* Treats DRAM and NVMe as peers, writing each block once in session-contiguous, layer-major order and streaming SSD reads into a GPU window with GPUDirect Storage; on a production Claude Code trace with DeepSeek-V4-Flash it sustains a 94% cache hit rate against 64% for Mooncake.
- **KVStream: Adaptive Parallel Storage Management for KV Caching in Large-Scale LLM Serving.** Avinash Maurya, Jaiaid Mobin, Jie Ye et al., 2026 (22 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31827. *Complementary.* Reads each chunk from whichever tier (local NVMe or a parallel file system) is free first, which its performance model shows reaches the aggregate-bandwidth bound; up to 6.1 times lower mean time to first token under bursty arrivals, plugged into LMCache. Relevant to Modal, where a local disk and a network Volume could serve together.
- **Tutti: Making SSD-Backed KV Cache Practical for Long-Context LLM Serving.** Shi Qiu, Yifan Hu, Xintao Wang et al., 2026 (5 May). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03375. *Complementary.* Moves I/O initiation onto the GPU because fragmented KV layouts produce many tiny reads that keep even GPUDirect Storage CPU-bound; against GDS-enabled SSD-backed LMCache it reports 78.3% lower time to first token under strict SLOs.
- **Compositional Performance Modeling of LLM Inference Clusters with Hierarchical KV Caching.** Ruiyang Ma, Sheng Qi, DaiKang Kuang et al., 2026 (11 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15366. *Closer (method).* A discrete-event simulator whose compute and transfer models, calibrated once on a one-prefill, one-decode deployment, are reused across workers without per-configuration fitting; validated against SGLang on Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B with mean end-to-end latency errors of 1.34% for question answering and 6.36% for coding sessions. Transfer across topologies; ours is transfer across attention designs.
- **SGLang HiCache and NVIDIA Dynamo's KV Block Manager.** Documentation, not checked for this review. *Baseline (systems).* The brief lists both as baselines; Gandalf cites the Dynamo KVBM guide at version 1.4.2.

### 2.7 Scheduling with a cache in the loop (the bridge to the first interest)

- **Fairway: Load-Aware KV Cache Reuse for LLM Prefill Scheduling.** Hamidreza Alikhani, Nazmul Takbir, Matin Khajavi et al., 2026 (1 November). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02082. *Complementary (scheduling).* Uncached work in a prefill queue can differ by up to 100 times between requests, so the order decides the time to first token: serving the longest cache hit first gives the best median but starves expensive requests under load spikes. Fairway favours reuse while queues are short and shifts toward fairness as requests wait, with a p99 up to 8.6 times lower than earlier prefix-aware schedulers. Our cost model supplies the per-request uncached work that such a scheduler ranks.
- **Vesta: Queueing-Aware LLM Scheduling for Real-World Workloads.** Lancheng Zou, Bowen Li, Wei Mao et al., 2026 (10 October). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14686. *Complementary (scheduling).* Chooses where each agent turn runs by weighing cache locality, KV transfer and the queueing externality of extra prefill work; 57.8% lower mean time to first token in the abstract. The load-dependent value of a hit, in scheduler form.
- **Token Latency Fairness: Performance Isolation for Multi-Tenant LLM Serving.** Dev Bali, Soujanya Ponnapalli, Yichuan Wang et al., 2026 (16 September). arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18112. *Complementary (multi-tenant SLOs).* FairInference bounds each well-behaved tenant's per-token delay relative to running alone, including delays from the shared KV cache. the student's first wish in its current form, and a starting point for a second paper.
- **Simple is Better: Multiplication May Be All You Need for LLM Request Scheduling.** Dingyan Zhang, Jinbo Han, Kaixi Zhang et al., 2026. OSDI 2026 (per the arXiv record). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15202. *Complementary (scheduling).* Routes by the product of the new prefill tokens a request would need on an instance and that instance's current batch size, with no tuning; from the Alibaba group that released the Bailian traces.
- **Preble: Efficient Distributed Prompt Scheduling for LLM Serving.** Vikranth Srivatsa, Zijian He, Reyna Abhyankar et al., 2024. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00023. *Complementary (scheduling).* Co-optimizes KV reuse and load balance across GPUs; the abstract reports 1.5 to 14.5 times lower average latency than the systems it compares with.
- **SGLang: Efficient Execution of Structured Language Model Programs.** Lianmin Zheng, Liangsheng Yin, Zhiqiang Xie et al., 2023. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07104. *Tool.* RadixAttention keeps reusable prefixes in a radix tree in GPU memory; SGLang's HiCache, which the brief names as a baseline, extends it beyond GPU memory.
- **PrefixShield** and **Continuum** (section 2.5) belong here too: one assigns cache pressure to the tenant that caused it, the other sets KV time-to-live from reload cost and queueing delay.

Gandalf used it and a sibling trace as one-day agent workloads (the duration is Gandalf's statement; we did not check it).
- **LongBench v2: Towards Deeper Understanding and Reasoning on Realistic Long-context Multitasks.** Yushi Bai, Shangqing Tu, Jiajie Zhang et al., 2024. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15204. *Testbed.* 503 long-context questions with contexts from 8k to 2M words; py-kvcache used it for chained prefixes.
- **SCBench: A KV Cache-Centric Analysis of Long-Context Methods.** Yucheng Li, Huiqiang Jiang, Qianhui Wu et al., 2024. ICLR 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319. *Testbed.* Shared-context, multi-request tasks that exercise KV generation, compression, retrieval and loading, including Mamba-attention hybrids.

## 3. The gap and our claim

- **Break-even models exist for one model and one tier at a time.** Ibrahim fits two terms for one MLA model on a remote memory tier; py-kvcache calibrates a break-even length per node, model and tier; EfficientAgent states the condition as compute per byte of host bandwidth for a DRAM tier; CacheFlow plans what to load and what to recompute across layer blocks. Nobody has tested whether one model, whose only per-design input is bytes per token, predicts the decision for attention designs it was not fitted on, on the same hardware.
- **Per-layer store-or-replay is well studied for capacity, not for time on a disk tier.** AnchorKV gives the storage break-even for hidden-state anchors from the config alone, HCache restores from hidden states, SuffixReplay and HiState handle linear-attention layers, and DeepSeek replays a bounded sliding window. Which option wins on a disk tier depends on bandwidth and recompute speed as well as bytes, and no paper puts these options under one time-and-dollar model.
- **Retention is studied as hit rate against capacity or as SSD wear, not as one price.** Working-set estimators, LRU analyses, production eviction studies and the M365 residency study measure hit rate against capacity; Gandalf, SARIA and Lachesis manage write endurance; DeepSeek sets 3- and 7-day TTLs from experience. A retention choice that prices capacity-time and endurance against recompute, per attention design, is not published.
- **Our claim, if Exp01 passes its gates.** On one GPU with a local SSD tier and a network tier, a cost model fitted on two attention designs predicts the load-or-recompute decision of the third in at least 19 of 21 (length, design) cells per tier, and its hit latency within 20%. With capacity-time and endurance prices added, the same model reproduces the published choices of DeepSeek V4.1 (persist global KV for days, keep sliding-window state for minutes, replay the rest), Gandalf (full-attention KV first) and AnchorKV (hidden-state anchors pay at every layer only for MHA-like shapes), and gives a cost-optimal retention per design for the minutes-to-a-day range that public traces cover. If the per-hit cost does not transfer across designs, the negative result, with its measurements, is itself the paper: break-even numbers in the literature would then be facts about implementations rather than about attention designs.

## 6. The first experiment (Exp01), run by the Vizuara team on days 1 to 4

**Exp01: measure the cost model's inputs on one Modal GPU and test whether bytes per token carries the load-or-recompute decision across MHA, GQA and MLA.** For a model it was not fitted on, does a cost model with four measured terms predict whether loading a cached prefix from disk beats recomputing it, at prompt lengths from 512 to 32k tokens, on Modal's local SSD and on a Modal Volume?

**The cost model under test.** For a reusable prefix of L tokens, with b bytes per token as stored:

- Replay costs T_rep(L) = aL + cL² seconds of GPU time (linear layers plus attention), fitted per model from cold prefill.
- A hit costs T_load(L) = T0 + k·n_chunks(L) + L·b/B, with a fixed per-hit cost T0 (lookup, scheduling, the forward pass over the uncached tail), a per-chunk cost k (Range shows that it matters) and an end-to-end bandwidth B from the tier into HBM.
- Persisting the prefix for a time τ costs L·b·(p_cap·τ + p_wear) dollars: capacity rented per byte-second, plus wear per byte written (the drive's price divided by its rated bytes written, multiplied by write amplification, after Gandalf).
- A hit is worth (T_rep − T_load) of GPU time when the load blocks the GPU, and up to T_rep when the load overlaps queueing; persisting pays when the expected number of hits within τ, times that value, exceeds the persistence cost.

Exp01 tests the first two lines across designs, measures the inputs of the third, and prices everything at Modal's list prices. Retention and per-layer choices come later (ablations A2 and A3, and weeks 3 to 7).

**What a Modal GPU container offers (read on 3 November; to be measured on day 1).** A worker-local SSD (512 GiB by default, up to 3.0 TiB through `ephemeral_disk`, gone when the container stops, speed undocumented) and Volumes (persistent network file system, designed for up to 2.5 GB/s, not guaranteed), both reached through a gVisor or microVM sandbox. Day 1 measures their real read bandwidth, whether `O_DIRECT` is honoured and how much the page cache hides.

**Models** (all ungated; revisions from the Hugging Face API on 3 November).

- MHA: `lmsys/longchat-7b-v1.5-32k` (revision 389eea14; Llama-2-7B shape, 32k context through linear RoPE scaling; 512 KiB per token; 13.5 GB of safetensors; download only those, because the repository also holds .bin copies).
- GQA: `Qwen/Qwen3-8B` (revision b968826d; 144 KiB per token, and 72 KiB with an FP8 cache, which halves b at unchanged compute, the cleanest test of the bytes term). Already in the Modal volume `irb15-exp01`.
- MLA: `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat` (revision 85864749; 16B total and 2.4B active parameters; 30.4 KiB per token; 31.4 GB).
- Synthetic load-side payloads of 890 bytes per token (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's persistent global KV) and 2,048 bytes per token. V4.1-Flash itself needs SM100 GPUs and does not fit one H100, so this point is a projection: its load side is measured and its recompute side is not.

**Workload.** Prompts of natural text (wikitext-103, so that DeepSeek-V2-Lite's router sees realistic tokens; licence not checked), cut to exact lengths with each model's tokenizer: 512, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k, 16k and 32k tokens, five distinct prompts per length, one output token, temperature 0. Every hit request repeats a stored prompt followed by 16 new tokens.

**Steps**.

1. Day 1, CPU containers. Create the Volume `irb20-exp01` (version 2). Download the LongChat safetensors and DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat at the pinned revisions; mount `irb15-exp01` read-only for Qwen3-8B. Build one image with vLLM 0.32.0, LMCache 0.5.6, fio and the analysis libraries, all pinned. Download the Mooncake conversation and tool-and-agent traces. Write `manifest.json` with revisions, file sizes, hashes and package versions.
2. Day 1, H100, 30 minutes: what storage does a Modal GPU container really have? Log `nvidia-smi`, `df -h`, `/proc/mounts` and the kernel string seen inside the sandbox, so that the GPU model and file-system types are on record. Request `ephemeral_disk=1048576` (1 TiB). With fio, write a 128 GiB file set on the ephemeral disk and a 32 GiB set on the Volume, then read them sequentially with 1 MiB and 16 MiB blocks at queue depths 1, 8 and 32, with `--direct=1` and buffered (try the libaio engine first and fall back to psync with several jobs if the sandbox rejects it). Cold-read check: read files written more than 64 GiB earlier and immediately re-read them; a re-read more than 1.5 times faster than the first read means the page cache is serving data, and all later timings then use the cold protocol (read only files outside the recently written window). Measure pinned host-to-GPU copy bandwidth with 256 MiB tensors. Save `storage.json`.
3. Day 1, same container. Start vLLM with each model for a two-minute cold prefill at 4k tokens to get a first prefill rate. From b (config), B (step 2) and that rate, write each model's predicted decision (load or recompute) at each length and tier into `decision_rule.json` before any hit is measured. This is the pre-registered prediction.
4. Day 2, H100, about 1.5 hours. For each model, run vLLM with prefix caching on, `max-model-len 32768` and LMCache's local-disk backend on the ephemeral disk (chunk size 256; `use_odirect` set by what step 2 found; LMCache's CPU tier disabled or cleared before each hit, so that hits are served from disk, which the LMCache log must confirm). For every prompt: (a) cold: record time to first token; (b) store: record the bytes on disk per token (from the cache directory, against the theoretical b) and the write time; (c) hit: reset vLLM's GPU prefix cache, apply the cold protocol, resend the prompt plus 16 tokens and record time to first token, hit tokens and bytes read. Repeat (c) with the cache directory on the Volume for Qwen3-8B, and repeat the whole sweep for Qwen3-8B with `--kv-cache-dtype fp8` if LMCache accepts that cache type (if not, record it and skip). If LMCache 0.5.6 does not run with vLLM 0.32.0, use vLLM's native KV offloading with its file-system tier and note the change.
5. Day 2, CPU container. Synthetic load side with our own reader in LMCache's chunk layout: 890 and 2,048 bytes per token, L from 1k to 1M tokens, timing disk reads; add the host-to-GPU copy on a GPU container only if CPU-side reads already exceed 2 GB/s.
6. Day 3, CPU (the H100 for at most 30 minutes, only to rerun cells whose coefficient of variation exceeds 10%). Fit a and c per model from cold runs. Fit T0, k and B on two models' hit runs and predict the third; rotate through all three. Compute decision accuracy, regret and hit-latency error, compare with the pre-registered predictions, and convert to dollars per million input tokens at Modal prices.
7. Day 4, CPU. Draw the figure, write `RESULTS.md`, check every number against `ttft.parquet` and the logs, and package the files for the ablations.

**Metric.** Primary: leave-one-design-out decision accuracy, the share of the 21 (length, design) cells per tier where the model picks the option that was measured faster, and the regret, the extra time to first token summed over wrong picks as a share of the total. Secondary: the mean absolute percentage error of predicted hit latency, and the break-even length where the measured curves cross between 512 and 32k tokens. Descriptive: measured B for the local SSD and the Volume, direct and buffered; T0 and k per model; bytes on disk per token against the config value.

**The one figure.** Three panels (MHA, GQA, MLA), time to first token against prompt length on log-log axes: measured recompute (circles) and disk hits (triangles, filled for the local SSD and open for the Volume), the leave-one-design-out prediction as a line and the pre-registered prediction as a dashed line, with the predicted break-even marked. An inset places each design (with Qwen3-8B's FP8 cache and the projected V4.1-Flash point) on the ratio of bytes per token to recompute time per token, against the measured bandwidths of the two tiers: the roofline view of persist or replay.

**What would confirm or kill the idea.** Our proposal, to be fixed with the project lead and written to `decision_rule.json` before step 4:

- *G0, the platform can be measured.* Cold reads on the ephemeral disk repeat within 15% over three runs, and the end-to-end bandwidth into HBM is at least 1 GB/s. If the sandbox prevents cold reads, or delivers less than 1 GB/s, the local-SSD arm does not represent a real server: we report the Volume arm, and the local-NVMe measurements move to a bare-metal host in week 2 (a budget decision for the project lead).
- *G1, the model transfers across designs.* Leave-one-design-out decision accuracy is at least 19 of 21 cells per tier, hit-latency error is at most 20%, and the fitted T0 varies by less than a factor of 1.5 across the three models. This confirms the paper's core claim.
- *Kill, or re-scope.* If T0 or B differs by more than a factor of 2 between models, the per-hit cost is implementation-specific and bytes per token does not carry the decision: the paper would become a calibrated per-model study close to py-kvcache, which is weak at this point, and we would recommend the scheduling angle of section 8 instead. If decision accuracy is below 15 of 21, the model is wrong in form: check the quadratic term, the chunk term and the overlap assumption before anything else.
- Anything in between is decided at the week-2 meeting, after the ablations.

## 8. Open scoping questions for the first discussion

- **Which question leads the paper?** After this review, RQ2 (which layers to store) is largely answered for capacity by AnchorKV, SuffixReplay, HiState and DeepSeek, and RQ3's endurance term by Gandalf and SARIA. We suggest that RQ1, the cross-design prediction in time and dollars, leads, with per-layer and retention results presented as applications that reproduce published choices. Is that narrower paper still the one we want to write?
- **Which "disk"?** On Modal the local SSD is ephemeral and the persistent option is a network Volume. A paper about multi-day retention needs a persistent tier, and a paper about NVMe bandwidth needs real local drives. We suggest both arms in Exp01, and a bare-metal NVMe host for a week in weeks 3 to 5 if G0 fails (a budget decision for the project lead).
- **Which metric replaces RQ1's target of predicting the break-even length within about 15%?** We propose decision accuracy and regret, with hit-latency error second (correction 10). Agreed?
- **How far to go with hybrid and sliding-window layers?** gpt-oss-20b (a sliding window of 128, like V4.1) and Qwen3.5-9B (Gated DeltaNet) are the natural testbeds, but support for persisting their state through vLLM 0.32 and LMCache 0.5.6 has to be checked first. If it is missing, should these designs enter only through the calculator (A3)?
- **Where does scheduling enter, and in which paper?** Two options: (a) in this paper, one load-aware rule, under which a hit's value includes the GPU time it frees when prefill is saturated, tested on a replayed trace at two load levels; (b) a second paper on SLO-aware persist-or-replay for multi-tenant serving, building on FairInference, PrefixShield, Fairway and Vesta, with this paper's measurements as its inputs. For the scheduling interest, (b) is the better fit; (a) keeps this paper short. If Exp01 fails G1, (b) becomes the main project.
- **Indian-language chats.** Keep them as a workload reported per word, and drop the clause from RQ3 (correction 12)?
- **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash itself.** One H100 can only project its point. An end-to-end measurement needs SM100 GPUs (DeepInfer's only NVIDIA target) and a multi-GPU deployment, well outside the bootcamp budget. Is a projection acceptable, with V4.1's own published choices as the check?
- **Retention beyond a day.** Public traces stop at one hour (Mooncake), two hours (Bailian) and one day (SemiAnalysis). Do we fit and extrapolate a reuse-time tail, say so, and compare with DeepSeek's single data point (a 3-day TTL costs chatbots 0.5% to 1% of hit rate), or restrict the claim to minutes-to-a-day?
- **Venue and timing.** HPAI4S (early February) and CHEOPS (mid-February) both have expected dates only; EuroMLSys (late February) is the fallback and HotStorage (mid-June) the last resort. Given the pace of this area, we suggest the earliest workshop whose 2027 call appears; check the calls in December.
- **Who reads it first.** The brief lists DeepSeek's inference team, the LMCache and Mooncake maintainers, SGLang's HiCache team, NVIDIA Dynamo and Indian clouds. The authors of AnchorKV, Gandalf and py-kvcache are now the closest readers and the most likely reviewers.
